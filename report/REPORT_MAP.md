## Findings

Each finding has a citable number.

- **F01 · [Women in every group](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F01)**: 68% are women. They are the majority in every age band and every region: 72% in Latin America, 67% in North America, 64% in Europe. Chart 1.90.
- **F02 · [Three regions, almost equal](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F02)**: North America and Latin America hold 31% each, Europe 23%. All other regions together hold 14%, which reflects who the survey reached. Chart 1.03.
- **F03 · [Brazil is the field's second home](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F03)**: By origin, Brazil (22%) is almost level with the United States (24%), well ahead of any other country. Chart 1.02.
- **F04 · [Late twenties and thirties](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F04)**: Half are 28 to 37 years old (52%); 28–32 is the largest band at 29%. Chart 1.04.
- **F05 · [One in five lives abroad](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F05)**: 20% live outside the country they come from and 14% changed region, mostly towards North America and Europe, where pay is higher. Chart 1.91.
- **F06 · [Identity beyond gender](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F06)**: Asked about underrepresented communities, 54% name women in tech and 19% LGBTQ+. Chart 1.06.
- **F07 · [A young field](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F07)**: 40% have three years or less in conversation design, and only 20% have seven or more. Chart 2.07.
- **F08 · [Promoted early](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F08)**: 62% of people with 4–6 years in the field are already senior or above. Overall, half the respondents (51%) are senior or more. Chart 2.90.
- **F09 · [Small or solo teams](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F09)**: 42% work alone or in a team of two or three conversation designers. Chart 2.14.
- **F10 · [Small teams feel less valued](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F10)**: 53% of solo designers feel valued at work, against 67% in teams of 21 or more. Chart 2.92.
- **F11 · [Remote by default](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F11)**: 60% are fully remote and only 2% work from an office. Europe is the exception, with 58% hybrid. Chart 2.91.
- **F12 · [One field, two pay markets](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F12)**: The middle half earns between $18k and $115k a year: a gap that mostly separates regions, not levels. Chart 2.93.
- **F13 · [Impact before recognition](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F13)**: 66% feel challenged to make a meaningful impact, but only 53% feel their work is valued. What would make it a 5: AI's effect on the role (37%), then recognition (33%). Chart 2.17.
- **F14 · [Place before level](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F14)**: A senior's median is $175k in North America and $23k in Latin America, 7.8 times more. Chart 9.12.
- **F15 · [The gender gap is mostly a region gap](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F15)**: Women's median is $43k against $65k for men overall, but $135k against $140k inside North America. Chart 9.13.
- **F16 · [Women thin out at the top](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F16)**: Women are 75% of mid-level roles and 38% of director, VP and C-level roles. Chart 9.11.
- **F17 · [Valued grows with level and team](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F17)**: Feeling valued rises from 3.13 at entry level to 3.66 at principal, and to 3.73 in teams of 21 or more (scale 1 to 5). Chart 9.02.
- **F18 · [Solo in smaller companies](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F18)**: 35% of people in companies of up to 200 employees are the only conversation designer, against 7% in companies of 5,000 or more. Chart 2.14.
- **F19 · [Valued and growth go together](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F19)**: Among those who feel valued (4 or 5), 70% also see clear career growth; among those who do not (1 or 2), only 30% do. Chart 2.16.
- **F20 · [Moving pays, in Europe](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F20)**: In Europe, people who came from another country earn a median of $65k against $48k for those who did not move (25 and 42 people). In North America the newcomers earn less: $115k against $135k. Chart 1.91.
- **F21 · [One in four works for clients](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F21)**: 27% worked in an agency or as freelancers in the past year. Chart 2.09.
- **F22 · [Most work across borders](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F22)**: 42% work globally, across countries or regions. Chart 2.12.
- **F23 · [A title takes hold](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F23)**: Almost half (47%) hold the title Conversation Designer; the next title, UX Writer or Content Designer, has 14%. Chart 3.18.
- **F24 · [Voice is regional](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F24)**: Chat is near universal (81%). Voice is common in North America (58%) and Europe (52%) but rare in Latin America (16%). Chart 3.33.
- **F25 · [Few work across channels](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F25)**: Only 28% work with both chat and voice; multimodal work (19%) is still a niche. Chart 3.33.
- **F26 · [Valued for experience, then for business](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F26)**: 69% deliver value by improving engagement and satisfaction, 59% by aligning flows to KPIs, 50% by lowering support costs. Chart 3.20.
- **F27 · [Close to product, far from leadership](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F27)**: 86% work often or daily with product and tech teams; only 33% with leadership. Chart 3.35.
- **F28 · [Agent Builders earn the most](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F28)**: The Agent Builder median is $95k, against $23k for the Flow Craftsperson. Half of Agent Builders (52%) live in North America, against 20% of Craftspeople. Chart A.pay.
- **F29 · [Optimizers are almost all women](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F29)**: 89% of Optimizers are women, against 55% of Agent Builders and Bridge Builders. Chart A.table.
- **F30 · [Learned on the job](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F30)**: 88% say on-the-job learning helped them most. Formal post-secondary programs help only 13%. Chart 4.27.
- **F31 · [Degrees, but not in conversation design](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F31)**: 52% have a degree in a related field, and linguistics or languages is named by 43% of those who listed their studies. Chart 4.22.
- **F32 · [Defined by what it is not](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F32)**: Asked what it is not, 54% say it is not just copywriting or scripting, and 32% not just chatbots. Chart 4.25.
- **F33 · [GenAI is now a mainstream skill](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F33)**: 47% already specialise in generative AI; agentic AI (17%) and AI orchestration (9%) are the next frontier. Chart 4.26.
- **F34 · [Its value, in two languages](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F34)**: People justify the work by user experience (46%) and by cost savings (35%). Chart 4.24.
- **F35 · [Communities teach](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F35)**: Communities and groups are the most cited learning resource (41%), ahead of courses (34%). Chart 4.28.
- **F36 · [Seniority moves the work to strategy](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F36)**: 60% at principal level and above specialise in strategy, against 9% at entry level. Chart 9.04.
- **F37 · [Juniors ask for training](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F37)**: 59% at entry level ask for better training, against 36% at principal level and above. Chart 9.09.
- **F38 · [Diagrams and spreadsheets](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F38)**: 77% use flow diagrams and 58% spreadsheets; Google Sheets is the most named tool (45%), ahead of Figma (30%). Chart 5.31n.
- **F39 · [Little code](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F39)**: Only 10% use code editors and 24% NLP training tools. Chart 5.30.
- **F40 · [Constraints, more than AI](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F40)**: The top challenge is quality under project constraints (46%), ahead of AI and NLP limits (35%). Chart 5.32.
- **F41 · [Value is hard to show](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F41)**: 34% struggle to communicate their value and 29% to measure success. Chart 5.32.
- **F42 · [No shared toolkit](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F42)**: Every widely used tool splits the archetypes by 56 points or more. Chart 5.92.
- **F43 · [Web and voice, level](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F43)**: Web chat and voice or IVR tie as the main channel, each at 34%. Chart 5.34.
- **F44 · [The GenAI year](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F44)**: 63% name adopting GenAI and LLMs as their biggest challenge of 2024, and 75% name it as what excited or worried them most. Chart 6.36.
- **F45 · [Tools ran ahead of understanding](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F45)**: 60% say tools improved in 2024; only 40% say businesses understand the value of the work better. Chart 6.38.
- **F46 · [Opportunities lag](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F46)**: Only 40% saw better career opportunities in 2024. Chart 6.38.
- **F47 · [Four readings of AI](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F47)**: A shift to prompting (32%), less demand for traditional skills (27%), new ethics risks (23%), a bigger role (19%): no consensus. Chart 6.39.
- **F48 · [Newcomers worry more about trust](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F48)**: 28% of those with up to 3 years say AI's main effect is new ethics and trust risks, against 18% of those with 7 or more. Chart 9.06.
- **F49 · [Certain change, uncertain demand](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F49)**: 77% expect AI to change the field significantly in 2025; 53% expect demand for designers to grow. Chart 7.91.
- **F50 · [Europe is the least sure](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F50)**: 43% in Europe expect demand to grow, against 56–57% in the Americas. Chart 7.45.
- **F51 · [AI literacy is the new baseline](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F51)**: 49% name AI and LLM literacy as essential for 2025, far ahead of any other skill. Chart 7.40.
- **F52 · [Goodbye rigid flows](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F52)**: 33% expect less rule-based bots and 28% less manual scripting. Chart 7.42.
- **F53 · [Not mature yet](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F53)**: 43% call the field fragmented and 38% evolving without standards; 5% call it established. Chart 7.44.
- **F54 · [Training first](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F54)**: Better training is the improvement asked for most (50%), then stakeholder support (44%) and better pay (40%). Chart 7.43.
- **F55 · [How you read AI predicts your outlook](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F55)**: Expected demand growth averages 4.00 among those who say AI expanded the role and 2.95 among those who say it reduced demand. Chart 9.10.
- **F56 · [Long, by feeling](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F56)**: 66% found the survey long, though the typical respondent took 25 minutes. Chart 8.50.
- **F57 · [They want the results](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F57)**: 87% are interested in the results. Chart 8.47.
- **F58 · [Length does not deter](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F58)**: 48% of those who found it long would take part again, close to 53% of those who found it right. Chart 8.47.
- **F59 · [Two waves](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F59)**: Half the answers came in the first four weeks, a second wave in the last two. Chart 8.51.
- **F60 · [Time given](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/findings/F60)**: Together, respondents gave about 119 hours to the survey. Chart 8.51.

## Archetypes

| Archetype | In short |
|---|---|
| [The Flow Craftsperson](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/archetypes/craft) | Shapes the conversation itself: flows, scripts, interface. Works on the conversation as an experience: the UX, the flow, the script. |
| [The Script Writer](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/archetypes/writer) | Writes the words, and increasingly the training data. Starts from UX writing, scripts and flows, and has added generative AI and model training to the toolkit. |
| [The Bridge Builder](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/archetypes/bridge) | Connects product, business and craft. Combines strategy, service design and project and stakeholder management with solid conversation UX. |
| [The Model Trainer](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/archetypes/trainer) | Trains and tunes the model behind the dialogue. The strongest on AI and NLP training, generative AI and data analysis. |
| [The Optimizer](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/archetypes/optimizer) | Reads the data and tunes the conversation. Spends the time on data, analysis and optimisation, and ties the work to conversion and measurable results. |
| [The Strategist](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/archetypes/strategist) | Frames the problem and carries it to the organisation. Works on strategy and roadmaps, research and testing, and service design, less on writing the dialogue itself. |
| [The Agent Builder](https://stateofconversationdesign.com/2025/archetypes/agent) | Builds with models, prompts and agents. Specialises in generative and agentic AI, writes prompts and orchestrates tools. |
